Multiplayer AR FPS games exist on Meta Quest, but you need to fiddle with room mapping, spatial anchors, and IP addresses/room codes. Stuff that makes hopping into a game with friends a chore.

With Lasertag (a lazy project folder name that accidentally stuck) I've gone to extremes to streamline onboarding and UX, including writing my own environment mapper that runs while you play instead of relying on Meta's painstaking room setup process.

Continuous environment scanning

This all works on the GPU. I take depth frames from Meta's DepthAPI and integrate them into a TSDF. The TSDF is semi-sparse with a small lookup table volume that indexes 3D world coordinates to the TSDF chunks. This all takes around 128 MB of memory.

I then mesh the volume on the CPU with a surface-net job + a third party mesh decimation job. Decimation ensures triangle counts are low enough for rendering the chunks in a depth-occlusion pass and using them for nav-mesh generation.

I'd eventually like to use the TSDF for multiplayer localization and drift correction, but I'm still getting a handle on some necessary math.

I still need to properly profile all of this, but it runs pretty efficiently at a glance. Lasertag holds a steady 72fps during play with CPU and GPU usage hovering around high 40s to low 50s. Any GPU spikes are usually due to the depth lights...
